Questions that come first
  • What does financial independence look like after the business?
  • How much of the family’s wealth is concentrated in the company?
  • What does your family want life to look like after the business is sold?

Preparation creates the ability to make thoughtful decisions before circumstances begin making them for you.

Phase 2 · Transition Coordination

The business may be for sale.
Your life is not.

A completed transaction and a prepared owner are not the same thing.

A business transition rarely involves one decision or one professional. Tax consequences influence liquidity. Liquidity influences investment decisions. Transaction structure can affect family wealth. Family expectations can affect what comes next.

Decisions made during the transaction can have consequences long after the closing documents are signed. Important transitions involve many professionals and many moving pieces; SaleReady helps keep responsibilities, decisions and next steps visible so important work does not quietly fall between them.

Decisions that stay connected
  • Business transition
  • Tax and structure
  • Liquidity
  • Family wealth
  • What comes next

Coordination is knowing how the pieces fit.

Follow-through is helping ensure the pieces keep moving.

Phase 3 · Family Wealth Stewardship

From operating wealth
to family wealth

The business may become liquidity.
The responsibility remains.

Stewardship of the wealth that follows a transition begins before the proceeds arrive.

For decades, the owner may have understood their principal asset intimately: the company, its people, its risks, its cash flow and its opportunities.

After a transition, that concentrated operating asset can become liquid family capital that may need to support the family for decades and create conversations that were never necessary while the wealth remained inside the company.

The owner may also be navigating a change in identity, purpose and responsibility after years or decades of building. SaleReady continues alongside the owner, the family and their other professional advisors.

A successful transition should not be measured only by the value realized at closing. It should also be measured by the preparedness of the people responsible for stewarding that value afterward.

Where to begin

The goal is not simply to sell well.
It is to be ready for what follows.

A business can take decades to build and months to sell. If a sale may be possible within the next three years, it is not too early to begin.

Before a transaction
There is time to prepare.

An owner is considering a sale, or believes one may be possible within the next few years. Preparation can begin with the owner, the family, the business and the wealth that follows, before urgency sets the timetable.

When a transaction is already moving
There is still time to coordinate.

An offer, a process or a transaction has already arrived. Tax, liquidity, family and investment decisions can still be coordinated around the work already underway, so the transaction and the life that follows are considered together.
